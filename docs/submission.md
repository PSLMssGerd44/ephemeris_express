Team: **Ephemeris Express Team — Paulina Sandoval**

## Summary 

Ephemeris Express is a browser-based mission-planning tool that answers the two questions that decide every lunar south-pole landing: when does a site have sunlight (power), and when can it see Earth (communication)? Built for the Lunar Landing Site Navigator challenge, it computes Sun and Earth positions for any lunar site and date with a physics core validated against NASA/JPL Horizons to 0.004° — including lunar librations, the finite solar disk, and topocentric parallax. NASA's LOLA laser-altimeter elevation model is integrated structurally: 360-azimuth terrain horizon masks correct every illumination and comms metric, and a 3D terrain view renders real south-polar topography with computed, time-accurate shadows. Mission planners can compare all Artemis IV candidate regions in one click, scrub decades of dates instantly, and back-compute the Earth-departure picture — ΔV versus time of flight and launch windows from six real launch sites. A built-in guide and a live plain-language interpreter make the same tool useful to engineers, educators, and the public. Everything runs client-side, free and open source.


## Project Details 
Ephemeris Express evaluates lunar landing sites in seconds. Pick a site – any of the 13 curated sites (Artemis candidate regions, CLPS and international robotic landings, Apollo) or custom coordinates – and a date. The tool shows the Sun and Earth in the local sky, countdowns to sunset/Earthset, 30-day illumination and direct-to-Earth communication statistics, a scrubbable timeline, a 3D view with a physically correct terminator, and – for south-polar sites – real LOLA terrain with computed shadows. A comparison table scores every site over the same window, and a mission planner estimates trans-lunar injection ΔV, C3, arrival v-infinity, LOI, and plane-alignment launch windows for six launch sites.
The core insight: Sun/Earth direction vectors, which are expressed in the Moon's body-fixed ME frame are site-independent, so one cached ephemeris time-series serves every site – az/el at any coordinates is a single rotation, making comparison effectively free, with no server and no API calls. Positions come from an analytic planetary ephemeris plus the IAU WGCCRE lunar orientation model with all 13 libration terms; topocentric parallax and the Sun's 0.53° disk are modeled; there is no atmospheric refraction, correctly. The pipeline that processes the LOLA LDEM_80S_80M polar DEM verifies its projection against four known landmarks and even snapped our rim sites to the true LOLA elevation crests – it ended up catching one of our own site guesses sitting 3 km deep inside Amundsen crater.
Every angle is validated against NASA/JPL Horizons (DE441): maximum residual 0.004° azimuth / 0.001° elevation – about 1/130th of the solar disk. The validation re-runs in CI on every commit; the site cannot deploy if the physics drifts. A year-long geometric invariant suite (polar Sun within ±1.6°, librations within ±8°, synodic rates) guards the frame chain.

The challenge notes that mission planners must re-evaluate sites quickly when schedules slip, and that existing tools are hard to use for rapid comparison. Ephemeris Express makes that evaluation instant and visual, and the same physics teaches: select IM-2 Athena and the tool shows the harsh lighting at the exact spot where that lander froze; the far-side Chang'e 4 shows Earth permanently below the horizon, that is why relay satellites exist. A built-in guide, glossary, hover explanations, and a live "What this means" interpreter translates every number into mission language for students and the public.
The tools that were used included: JavaScript (ES modules), Three.js, Vite, astronomy-engine; Python/NumPy for the offline LOLA pipeline; GitHub Actions CI with a validation gate; GitHub Pages hosting. The NASA data was retrieved from: LRO LOLA GDR (PDS), JPL Horizons, Artemis III candidate regions. Fully open source.
Additionally, the site-independent ephemeris architecture, terrain shadows that are computed rather than painted, landmark-verified data processing, a validation-gated deploy pipeline, and the flat-vs-terrain comparison insight (terrain "helps" summit sites and "hurts" plateau sites) are, to our knowledge, unique among rapid site-evaluation tools.
